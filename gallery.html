Marine Biodiversity

4 species
Click to explore ↗
Sea Turtles ▼

These ancient mariners are essential for maintaining healthy seagrass beds and coral reefs. By grazing on seagrass, they ensure the ecosystem remains productive and provides a nursery for numerous fish species.

Click to explore ↗
Whales ▼

As the giants of the deep, whales play a crucial role in the ocean's carbon cycle. Their movements help circulate nutrients throughout the water column, supporting the growth of phytoplankton which produces half of Earth's oxygen.

Click to explore ↗
Coral Reefs ▼

Coral reefs are biodiversity hotspots, supporting approximately 25% of all marine species despite covering less than 1% of the ocean floor. They provide critical habitat for fish and other marine life, and also protect coastlines from erosion and storm damage.

Click to explore ↗
Jellyfish ▼

Jellyfish are gelatinous marine animals that play a significant role in ocean ecosystems. They serve as both predators and prey, helping to control plankton populations and providing a food source for larger marine animals.

Human Impacts

3 threats
Click to explore ↗
Plastic Pollution ▼

Plastic pollution in the oceans poses a severe threat to marine life. It can cause injury and death to animals through ingestion or entanglement, and also disrupts the balance of marine ecosystems.

Click to explore ↗
Ghost Nets ▼

Abandoned or lost fishing gear, known as ghost nets, continues to "fish" for decades. These invisible traps kill thousands of turtles, dolphins, and sharks who become hopelessly entangled in the mesh.

Click to explore ↗
Bleached Corals ▼

Coral bleaching occurs when corals expel the algae (zooxanthellae) living in their tissues due to stress from increased water temperatures. This process can lead to the death of coral reefs, which are vital for marine biodiversity.

Conservation Efforts

3 actions
Click to explore ↗
Coral Restoration ▼

Dedicated marine scientists are "planting" lab-grown coral fragments back onto damaged reefs. This active restoration helps jumpstart the recovery of ecosystems that have been devastated by warming waters.

Click to explore ↗
Beach Clean-ups ▼

Local volunteer groups remove thousands of pounds of trash from shorelines before it can wash into the sea. These grassroots efforts prevent immediate harm to coastal birds and nesting sea turtles.

Click to explore ↗
Marine Protected Areas ▼

Establishing marine protected areas helps preserve critical habitats and allows marine ecosystems to recover from human impacts. These zones restrict certain activities like fishing or development, giving marine life a chance to thrive.
